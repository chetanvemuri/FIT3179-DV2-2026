Why Australians travel

Australians spent 89 million nights away from home on overnight trips in the June quarter of 2026. Holidays made up the largest share, but almost 4 in 10 nights were spent visiting friends and relatives, often sleeping in a spare room rather than paying for a bed.

The visitors still missing

About 711,000 overseas visitors arrived in Australia in July 2026. That is still 10% fewer than in July 2019, before COVID closed the borders. The gap is not the same everywhere: Western Australia, New South Wales and Queensland are above 90% of their old numbers, while Tasmania, the Northern Territory and the ACT have recovered only around three quarters.

Where our visitors come from

In July 2019, China sent more visitors to Australia than any other country. In July 2026, New Zealand took the top spot with about 134,000 visitors, while visitors from China fell by around a fifth to 117,000.

Where the tourist dollar goes

In 2024–25, tourists in Australia spent $21.8 billion on paid accommodation. That is less than they spent on takeaway and restaurant meals, flights, groceries or fuel. Holiday homes add another $15.6 billion, which includes the rent owners would have paid to stay in their own holiday house.
