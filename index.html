Every night, hundreds of thousands of people in Australia sleep away from home. This page follows those nights to see how travel is shaping where we stay.

01 How we travel

Australians on the move

Why Australians travel

Holidays fill half of our 89 million travel nights, but almost 4 in 10 are spent with friends and family, often in a spare room rather than a paid bed.

Where the tourist dollar goes

Tourists spent $21.8 billion on paid accommodation in 2024–25, less than on meals, flights, groceries or fuel.

02 Visitors from overseas

The world comes back, slowly

The visitors still missing

July 2026 brought 711,000 overseas visitors, still 10% short of July 2019. WA, NSW and Queensland are above 90%; Tasmania, the NT and the ACT are near three quarters.

When visitors come

Most states peak in summer, and Tasmania nearly doubles in February. The Northern Territory is the exception: its dry-season winter is the busiest time of year.

Where our visitors come from

New Zealand has overtaken China, whose visitors fell by a fifth. Taiwan (+39%) and South Korea (+21%) are the fastest growers.

03 Where we stay

Hotels, homes and everything between

The Airbnb map of Melbourne

Melbourne has 25,715 Airbnb listings. A third are in the City of Melbourne, and many more line the bay from St Kilda to Frankston. Almost 3 in 4 are entire homes.

Whole homes in the city and the hills

In the inner city, by the bay and in the Yarra Ranges, over 80% of listings are whole homes. In Dandenong and Wyndham, more locals rent out a spare room.

What a night costs

A typical entire home costs $302 a night; a private room costs $109.

Not just spare rooms

44% of listings belong to hosts with just one, but one in three belongs to a host running six or more. For them, short stays are a business.

A good year for hotels

Despite fewer overseas visitors, nine of the ten biggest hotel markets earned more per room in early 2026, led by the Sunshine Coast. Only Canberra slipped, and only just.

04 What it means

The bottom line

1

Recovery is uneven. Overseas visitors are still 10% down, most in Tasmania, the NT and the ACT.

2

Growth comes from Asia. Korea and Taiwan are rising while China and the US lag.

3

Stays are changing. Airbnbs are mostly whole homes, and hotels are earning more per room.
